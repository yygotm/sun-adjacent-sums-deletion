# Four-element deletion in odd finite abelian groups

## 1. Statement and preliminary results

**Theorem.** Let $G$ be a finite abelian group of odd order $N\ge7$,
and let $D\subseteq G$ have four elements. There is a cyclic ordering
of $G\setminus D$ with pairwise distinct adjacent sums, including
the sum of the last and first vertices.

There are no exceptions within this range. At the boundary $N=7$,
three vertices remain, and their three pair sums are distinct by
cancellation. Orders three and five do not satisfy $N-4>2$.

Call a cyclic ordering **rainbow** when its adjacent sums are distinct,
and denote its color set by $S(C)$. Multiplication by two is a
bijection on an odd finite abelian group and on every quotient of it.
An affine isomorphism $x\mapsto t+L(x)$ takes colors to
$s\mapsto2t+L(s)$, so preserves the rainbow property.

We use these proved results:

- **Prescribed-edge property:** every pair of distinct elements of a
  nontrivial odd finite abelian group $H$ is an edge of a full rainbow
  cycle. Consequently, any distinct $x,y\in H$ are endpoints of a
  spanning path whose internal colors are exactly
  $H\setminus\{x+y\}$. Translation gives the corresponding coset
  version. See **two-deletion.md, Sections 1 and 3**.
- **Subgroup extension:** if $B\subseteq H\le G$, $H$ is nontrivial,
  and $B$ has a rainbow cycle of length at least three, then
  $(G\setminus H)\cup B$ has a rainbow cycle with color set
  $(G\setminus H)\cup S(B)$. See **two-deletion.md, Section 4**.
- **Two-element deletion:** if $|H|\ge5$, deleting any two elements
  from $H$ leaves a rainbow cycle. See **two-deletion.md, Section 5**.
- **Three-element deletion:** if $|H|\ge7$, deleting any three
  elements from $H$ leaves a rainbow cycle. See
  **three-deletion.md, Sections 1-10**.

The prescribed-edge property and subgroup extension are established
independently of the deletion theorems. The three-deletion result uses
a bounded certificate of 525 explicit cyclic lists, described and
verified in **three-deletion.md, Section 7**; this is also a dependency
of the present theorem. No small-group experiment is used to infer an
unproved infinite family.

## 2. Gluing with one full fiber

**Lemma 2.1.** Let $H<K$ be odd finite abelian groups with
$|H|=h$, and suppose $K/H$ is cyclic of odd order $q\ge3$,
generated by $r+H$. Fix $t\in K$ and a set $D\subseteq K$ of
$k<h$ holes. Suppose that:

1. the fiber $t+(q-1)r+H$ contains no holes;
2. for every $0\le i<q-1$, the set
   $B_i=\{z\in H:t+ir+z\notin D\}$ has a specified rainbow
   cycle $C_i$ of length at least three.

Then $K\setminus D$ has a rainbow cycle with color set

\[
\left(\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{q-2}\bigl(2t+2ir+S(C_i)\bigr)\right)
\sqcup\bigl(2t+2(q-1)r+H\bigr).
\tag{2.1}
\]

The construction retains all uncut internal edges of the local cycles.
It applies to arbitrary $k$ satisfying the hypotheses; it does not
require $H$ to be a direct factor of $K$.

### 2.1 Choosing admissible endpoints

Fix an orientation of each $C_i$. Its predecessor map $f_i$ is a
permutation of $B_i$; extend $f_i$ to a permutation of $H$ by fixing
every element of $H\setminus B_i$. For a proposed starting value
$u\in H$, put $z_0=u$ and $z_{i+1}=f_i(z_i)$. Each map
$u\mapsto z_i$ is a permutation of $H$. Therefore exactly
$|H\setminus B_i|$ starting values fail the condition $z_i\in B_i$.
The union of all forbidden starting values has size at most

\[
\sum_{i=0}^{q-2}|H\setminus B_i|=k<h.
\]

Choose $u$ for which all these membership conditions hold. For
$i<q-2$, rotate $C_i$ to start at $z_i$ in its fixed orientation.
As a path its endpoint is $z_{i+1}$.

For $C_{q-2}$, keep the start $z_{q-2}$ but allow either orientation.
The two possible endpoints $w_+,w_-$ are the two neighbors of the
start. They are distinct because the cycle has at least three vertices.
At most one equals $u-qr$. Choose the orientation with endpoint
$w\ne u-qr$. This choice changes no earlier start or endpoint.
Since the last fiber is full, no further membership condition is needed.
Also $qr\in H$, so $w,u-qr\in H$. The prescribed-edge property
provides a spanning path of $H$ from $w$ to $u-qr$.

### 2.2 Restoring colors at all joins

Translate the relative-coordinate paths into their fibers
$t+ir+H$. Denote their actual endpoints by $x_i,y_i$. They satisfy

\[
x_{i+1}=y_i+r\quad(0\le i<q-1),\qquad x_0=y_{q-1}+r.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The closing identity uses the final relative endpoint $u-qr$:

\[
y_{q-1}=t+(q-1)r+(u-qr)=t+u-r=x_0-r.
\tag{2.3}
\]

This is the correction required when the chosen lift $r$ does not
have order $q$ in $K$. No assertion $qr=0$ is made.

Put $s_i=x_i+y_i$. For $i<q-1$, cutting $C_i$ removes precisely
$s_i$ from its translated color set. For the full last fiber, its
internal path colors are its entire color coset with $s_{q-1}$ removed.
Concatenate the blocks in order $0,2,4,\ldots$ modulo $q$.
Since $q$ is odd, this visits every block once. By (2.2), each join
has color

\[
y_i+x_{i+2}=y_i+y_{i+1}+r=x_{i+1}+y_{i+1}=s_{i+1},
\tag{2.4}
\]

with indices modulo $q$. In particular, the two wrapping cases are

\[
y_{q-2}+x_0=s_{q-1},\qquad y_{q-1}+x_1=s_0.
\]

The first is the last-to-first join in this block ordering. Thus every
reserved color is restored exactly once. The original color sets lie
in distinct cosets $2t+2ir+H$, because doubling is a permutation of
$K/H$. No restored color repeats an internal color, and no colors from
different fibers coincide. The blocks partition $K\setminus D$,
proving both the rainbow property and (2.1). $\square$

### 2.3 Applicability of the lemma

If $q>k$, at least one fiber is full, and changing $t$ puts that fiber
last. More generally, a full fiber can be supplied by a suitable choice
of quotient. The condition $h>k$ guarantees an admissible start;
the existence of the local cycles must still be proved separately.

For four holes and $h\ge7$, if the holes occupy at least two fibers,
each fiber has at most three holes. The established zero-, one-, two-,
and three-hole results then give all local cycles. The one-hole result
is proved in Section 4 below. If all four holes lie in one proper
subgroup coset, the induction in Section 8 instead applies four-deletion
inside that strictly smaller subgroup, followed by subgroup extension.

The lemma does not cover an empty or two-vertex retained fiber, and it
does not by itself guarantee the existence of a suitable proper
subgroup. The cyclic case is therefore treated separately.

## 3. Odd-cardinality subsets of cyclic groups

**Lemma 3.1.** Every odd-cardinality subset of a finite cyclic group,
with cardinality at least three, has a rainbow cyclic ordering. The
ambient cyclic group may have either parity.

**Proof.** Identify the group with $\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z$ and
choose increasing representatives of the subset,

\[
0\le a_0<a_1<\cdots<a_{2l}<M,\qquad l\ge1.
\]

Use the cyclic list

\[
a_0,a_l,a_{2l},a_{l-1},a_{2l-1},\ldots,a_1,a_{l+1}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Precisely, begin with $a_0,a_l$; for $j=l,l-1,\ldots,1$ append
$a_{l+j}$, followed by $a_{j-1}$ if $j>1$. This rule applies also
to $l=1$ and uses every index exactly once.

The integer edge sums of this list, including its closing edge, are
exactly the following $2l+1$ numbers, written in increasing order:

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_0&=a_0+a_l,\\
b_{2j+1}&=a_j+a_{l+j+1}\quad(0\le j<l),\\
b_{2j+2}&=a_{j+1}+a_{l+j+1}\quad(0\le j<l).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The closing edge has sum $a_{l+1}+a_0=b_1$. Each successive
comparison in (3.2) strictly increases one summand while keeping the
other fixed. Hence the list of sums is strictly increasing. Moreover,

\[
b_{2l}-b_0=(a_l+a_{2l})-(a_0+a_l)=a_{2l}-a_0<M.
\]

Two distinct sums in an interval of integer width less than $M$ cannot
be congruent modulo $M$. Thus all cyclic sums are distinct in the
group. $\square$

In particular, an odd-order cyclic group admits every even number of
deletions whenever at least three vertices remain. No finite
certificate or deletion induction is needed in this case.

## 4. The one-hole input

**Lemma 4.1.** If $H$ is an odd finite abelian group of order at least
five, then $H\setminus\{a\}$ has a rainbow cycle whose only missing
color is $2a$.

**Proof.** Translate so that $a=0$. If $H$ has an element $d$ of
order $m\ge5$, use the cycle $Q_m$ on
$\langle d\rangle\setminus\{0\}$, with exactly the nonzero colors,
proved in **three-deletion.md, Section 2**. Subgroup extension supplies
a cycle on $H\setminus\{0\}$ with precisely the nonzero colors.

Otherwise every nonzero element of $H$ has order three. Since
$|H|\ge5$, the group contains a subgroup $L\cong C_3^2$ with basis
$d,e$. Encode $id+je$ by $i+3j$, for $0\le i,j<3$. The cycle

\[
1,3,4,7,2,6,8,5
\]

has successive colors

\[
4,7,2,6,8,5,1,3,
\]

using coordinatewise addition modulo three. Thus it uses exactly the
nonzero vertices and colors of $L$. Subgroup extension again gives the
claim in $H$. Translation back by $a$ shifts the missing color from
zero to $2a$. $\square$

## 5. Choosing a quotient for a noncyclic 3-group

**Lemma 5.1.** Let $V$ be a vector space over $\mathbb F_3$ of
dimension at least two. For any four points, allowing repetitions,
there is a nonzero linear functional $\ell:V\to\mathbb F_3$
whose values on those points occupy at most two residues.

**Proof.** If $\dim V\ge3$, partition the four points into two
pairs. Their two pair differences span a subspace of dimension at
most two. A nonzero functional annihilating that subspace is constant
on each pair and therefore has at most two values on the four points.

Now suppose $\dim V=2$. If there are at most three distinct points,
annihilate the difference between two of them; this identifies two
images and leaves at most two values. If there is at most one distinct
point, any nonzero functional suffices.

If all four points are distinct, their six unordered pairs determine
six directions counted with repetition, among the four one-dimensional
subspaces of $\mathbb F_3^2$. Two distinct pairs have the same
direction. A nonzero functional with that direction as kernel
identifies both pairs. If the pairs are disjoint, they give two classes;
if they intersect, three points have the same image. In either case
at most two values occur. $\square$

For a noncyclic finite abelian 3-group $G$, the structure theorem gives
$\dim_{\mathbb F_3}(G/3G)\ge2$. Apply Lemma 5.1 to the images of
the four holes. Composing the resulting functional with $G\to G/3G$
gives an epimorphism $G\to C_3$ with a hole-free fiber. The kernel
need not be a direct factor; Lemma 2.1 accommodates this.

## 6. The group $C_5^2$

**Lemma 6.1.** Deleting any four points from $C_5^2$ leaves a rainbow
cycle.

**Proof.** The plane $\mathbb F_5^2$ has six line directions.
At most $\binom43=4$ of them contain a collinear triple of the four
holes: each such direction is the direction of at least one of the
four triples. Choose a direction avoiding them, and let $H$ be the
line subgroup in that direction. Each $H$-coset contains at most two
holes. There are five cosets and only four holes, so some coset is full.

Here $|H|=5>4$ and every retained fiber has at least three vertices.
The prescribed-edge property and the one- and two-deletion results
give the local cycles. The quotient is cyclic of order five, so
Lemma 2.1 applies. $\square$

## 7. The group $C_3^2$

**Lemma 7.1.** Deleting any four points from $C_3^2$ leaves a rainbow
cycle.

**Proof.** For a basis $d,e$, encode $id+je$ by $i+3j$ with
$0\le i,j<3$. The following four explicit lists are rainbow cycles
for the indicated standard hole sets:

| Hole indices | Cycle indices | Cyclic color indices |
|---|---|---|
| 0,1,2,3 | 4,5,6,7,8 | 6,2,4,3,0 |
| 0,1,3,4 | 2,5,6,8,7 | 4,2,5,3,6 |
| 0,1,3,5 | 2,4,7,6,8 | 3,2,4,5,7 |
| 0,1,3,7 | 2,4,5,6,8 | 3,6,2,5,7 |

All sums are computed coordinatewise modulo three, not by adding the
indices modulo nine. Each row contains exactly the five retained
vertices and five distinct colors, including its closing color.

It remains to prove that these rows cover all four-element hole sets
under affine changes of coordinates. If a hole set contains a line,
an affine change sends its three points to $0,d,2d$ and the fourth
point to $e$, giving the first row.

Otherwise any three holes are affinely independent; send them to
$0,d,e$. Write the fourth as $\alpha d+\beta e$. Avoiding the lines
through each pair of the first three points requires

\[
\alpha\ne0,\qquad \beta\ne0,\qquad \alpha+\beta\ne1.
\]

Over $\mathbb F_3$ the possibilities are exactly $(1,1),(2,1),(1,2)$,
encoded by $4,5,7$. These give the final three rows. An affine map
$x\mapsto t+L(x)$ maps colors injectively by $s\mapsto2t+L(s)$,
so every affine image of one of the listed cycles is also rainbow.
The four rows need not be distinct affine orbits; their proved coverage
is sufficient. $\square$

## 8. Proof of the four-deletion theorem

Proceed by strong induction on the odd group order $N\ge7$.
If $G$ is cyclic, Lemma 3.1 applies because $N-4$ is odd and at least
three. This includes $N=7$.

Suppose $G$ is noncyclic. The groups $C_3^2$ and $C_5^2$ are covered
by Lemmas 7.1 and 6.1. For every other noncyclic group, we will find
a proper subgroup $H$ such that:

\[
|H|\ge7,\qquad G/H\text{ is cyclic of odd prime order},
\qquad\text{some }H\text{-coset is hole-free}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

If some prime $p\ge5$ divides $N$, the structure theorem for finite
abelian groups supplies an epimorphism $G\to C_p$. Let $H$ be its
kernel, of order $h=N/p$. Since $p>4$, a fiber is hole-free.
The odd possibilities $h<7$ are $1,3,5$. They cannot occur in the
present branch:

- $h=1$ makes $G$ cyclic of prime order.
- $h=3$ makes $G$ an abelian group of squarefree order $3p$, hence
  cyclic.
- $h=5$ likewise gives a cyclic group of order $5p$ if $p\ne5$;
  if $p=5$, the only noncyclic abelian group of order $25$ is
  $C_5^2$, already treated.

Thus $h\ge7$. If there is no prime divisor at least five, $G$ is
a noncyclic 3-group. Apart from $C_3^2$, its order is at least $27$.
Section 5 gives an epimorphism $G\to C_3$ with a hole-free fiber;
its kernel has order $N/3\ge9$. This proves (8.1) in every case.

Now fix such an $H$. If all four holes lie in one coset $a+H$,
translate by $-a$. Apply the induction hypothesis to the group $H$
and its four translated holes. This is a legitimate invocation, since
$7\le|H|<|G|$. The retained cycle has at least three vertices.
Use subgroup extension to obtain a cycle on the complement of those
four holes in $G$, and then translate back.

If the holes occupy at least two fibers, each fiber has at most three
holes. For a full fiber use a full cycle from the prescribed-edge
property. For one, two, or three holes use Lemma 4.1 or the respective
proved deletion theorem in Section 1. Since $h\ge7$, each resulting
cycle has at least $h-3\ge4$ vertices. Also $h>4$, and a full fiber
is available by (8.1). Lemma 2.1 therefore joins the local cycles into
the desired rainbow cycle on $G\setminus D$.

These cases exhaust all odd finite abelian groups. Every invocation
of the four-deletion induction is on a strictly smaller proper
subgroup; all other inputs are independent results. Hence the induction
is valid and proves the theorem for all $N\ge7$. $\square$

## 9. Reusable parts and exact scope

The endpoint selection and gluing in Lemma 2.1 work for any number
$k$ of holes under the explicit assumptions $|H|>k$, one full fiber,
and suitable local cycles of length at least three. They preserve
the local color sets and every uncut internal edge, including when
the quotient does not split. Lemma 3.1 handles every odd-cardinality
cyclic subset, and therefore every even deletion count in an odd-order
cyclic group.

The quotient-selection arguments in Sections 5 and 6 use specifically
four holes. They do not prove arbitrary $k$-deletion in all odd finite
abelian groups, nor a prescribed missing-color formula. For example,
the five points in the union of two intersecting affine lines in
$\mathbb F_3^2$ meet every affine line: a line parallel to one of the
two intersects the other, and any line with a third direction
intersects both. Thus no quotient onto $C_3$ has a hole-free fiber for
that five-point set. The present full-fiber argument cannot apply to
it unchanged.
