# Distinct adjacent sums after deleting two, three or four elements of an odd-order abelian group

This repository contains proofs, Lean 4 formalizations and computer checks of three
special cases of a conjecture of Zhi-Wei Sun.

**Conjecture** (Z.-W. Sun, *Some new problems in additive combinatorics*,
arXiv:1309.1679, Conjecture 3.3(i); also Problem 21.130 of the Kourovka Notebook).
Let G be a finite abelian group of odd order and let A be a subset of G with n = |A| > 2.
Then the elements of A can be listed as a_1, ..., a_n so that the n sums

    a_1 + a_2, a_2 + a_3, ..., a_{n-1} + a_n, a_n + a_1

are pairwise distinct.

For A = G and A = G \ {0} (hence A = G \ {a}) this follows from the work of Beals, Gallian,
Headley and Jungreis on harmonious groups (J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 56 (1991) 223-238).

## Results

Let G be a finite abelian group of odd order and D a subset of G with |D| = k.

* **k = 2** (|G| >= 5). For distinct a, b in G, the set G \ {a, b} has such a cyclic
  ordering, and the ordering can be chosen so that its set of adjacent sums is exactly
  G \ {3a - b, 3b - a}.
* **k = 3** (|G| >= 7). For every 3-element D, G \ D has such a cyclic ordering.
* **k = 4** (|G| >= 7). For every 4-element D, G \ D has such a cyclic ordering.

So Sun's conjecture holds whenever |A| >= |G| - 4. There are no exceptional groups in these
ranges (for k = 3, 4 the bound |G| >= 7 is just the condition n > 2). The conjecture for
arbitrary subsets remains open.

| Result | Proof | Lean |
|---|---|---|
| k = 2 | [proofs/two-deletion.md](proofs/two-deletion.md) | `SunTwoDeletion.sun_two_deletion_exact`, `SunTwoDeletion.sun_two_deletion_ordering` |
| k = 3 | [proofs/three-deletion.md](proofs/three-deletion.md) | `SunThreeDeletion.sun_three_deletion` |
| k = 4 | [proofs/four-deletion.md](proofs/four-deletion.md) | `SunFourDeletion.sun_four_deletion` |
| \|A\| = 3, 4, 5; complements of a coset of a subgroup | [proofs/small-sets-and-coset-complements.md](proofs/small-sets-and-coset-complements.md) | not formalized |

All three proofs glue cyclic orderings of the cosets of a subgroup H along a generator
of a cyclic quotient G/H, keeping track of the endpoints and of the correction needed
when G/H does not split; full cosets use a prescribed-edge property of full cyclic
orderings that is proved in the documents themselves. The two- and four-deletion
proofs are inductions on |G| through such a subgroup. The three-deletion proof is
instead a case split on the subgroup generated by the differences of the deleted
elements, with explicit cyclic constructions (including a finite list of 525 explicit
cyclic sequences, which are checked in Lean); it uses the two-deletion results but not
itself for a smaller group.

## Where to start

* **Reading.** Start with [proofs/two-deletion.md](proofs/two-deletion.md) (Sections 2
  and 5 give the gluing construction and the proof); the three- and four-deletion
  arguments build on its tools. For a first pass through the others: the statement and
  Section 10 of [proofs/three-deletion.md](proofs/three-deletion.md) give its case
  split, and Sections 2 and 8 of [proofs/four-deletion.md](proofs/four-deletion.md)
  give its gluing lemma and induction.
  [proofs/small-sets-and-coset-complements.md](proofs/small-sets-and-coset-complements.md)
  is supplementary; its Section 6 shows how the existence statements for sufficiently
  large groups also follow from Theorem 6.9 of Müyesser and Pokrovskiy.
* **Quick check, Python only** (Python 3.9 or later, standard library). From the
  repository root:

  ```sh
  python3 -I code/three-deletion/verify_certificate.py
  python3 -I code/four-deletion/audit_proof_table.py
  ```

  On Windows use the installed interpreter, for example `py -3` or `python`, in place
  of `python3`. The first command checks the 525 explicit cycles used in the
  three-deletion proof and prints `{"cases": 525, "cyclicEdges": 15680, "pass": true}`;
  the second checks the base cases printed in `proofs/four-deletion.md` and prints a
  line beginning `{"pass": true` (it also writes `proof-table-audit.json` to the current
  directory). Each took well under a second in our tests. They check
  finite base cases only, not the general theorems.
* **A small exhaustive search** (.NET SDK 10.0 and Python), all subsets of the groups of
  order at most 9, from `code/all-subsets32` in a fresh clone:

  ```sh
  dotnet build SunCheck.csproj -c Release
  dotnet bin/Release/net10.0/SunCheck.dll --selftest
  dotnet bin/Release/net10.0/SunCheck.dll --groups Z3,Z5,Z7,Z9,Z3xZ3 --threads 1
  python3 -I ../audit_all_subsets.py runs
  ```

  The audit should report 132 representatives, 563 zero-containing subsets and 1,048
  subsets in all. Build and run took about 25 seconds on Windows. Results are resumed
  from `runs`, so use a fresh clone or output directory; the full commands are in
  Section 5 of [computation.md](computation.md).
* **The Lean proofs.** Install Lean and its toolchain manager elan by following the
  [official installation guide](https://lean-lang.org/install/manual/) (Git is also
  needed, and `lake` must be on the PATH). Then, from the root of the cloned repository,
  run the three commands in [Formal verification](#formal-verification) below;
  `lean-toolchain` selects the right Lean version automatically. The first run downloads
  Mathlib and its precompiled cache and so needs network access; we have not measured
  its total time or disk use. The expected output is described in Section 7 of
  [lean.md](lean.md).
* **The full exhaustive searches** are described in [computation.md](computation.md).
  Some large witness and summary files are not included (Section 7 there), so
  `code/four-deletion/summarize.py` and `audit.py` run only after those files are
  regenerated with the .NET programs. The second implementation in
  `code/exhaustive-claude/` is a C program whose `run.sh` targets Linux (GCC and GNU
  time).

## Formal verification

The Lean sources prove the three statements above with no `sorry` and no additional
axioms (`#print axioms` gives only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`).
From the repository root:

```sh
lake exe cache get
lake build
lake env lean Audit.lean
```

Details, including the exact statements, are in [lean.md](lean.md).

## Computation

Independently of the proofs, an exhaustive search found such an ordering for every subset
A with |A| >= 3 of every abelian group of odd order at most 35 (21 groups); no
counterexample. Orders up to 31 were also checked by a second, independent implementation
(`code/exhaustive-claude/`). For k = 4, every 4-element deletion was also checked directly
in all 31 abelian groups of odd order 7 to 55. Details are in
[computation.md](computation.md).

## Prior work and novelty

Sun (arXiv:1811.10503, version 6) reports that Yu-Xuan Ji checked the conjecture above
(Conjecture 3.3(i) of arXiv:1309.1679) by computer in 2020 for all abelian groups of odd
order less than 30. The exhaustive search described in the Computation section repeats
this check and extends it to all odd orders at most 35.

We have not found the deletion statements in the literature. We searched the papers citing Sun's
arXiv:1309.1679 and Beals et al. (1991), the papers citing Müyesser and Pokrovskiy,
*A random Hall-Paige conjecture* (Invent. Math. 2025), and the survey of M. A. Ollis,
*Sequenceable groups and related topics* (Electron. J. Combin. Dynamic Survey DS10,
version 3, 2025). For groups that are large compared with k, the existence part of the k-deletion statement
can also be derived from Theorem 6.9 of Müyesser and Pokrovskiy (no explicit bound on |G|
is given there); the proofs here are elementary and cover all orders. We do not claim
that the results are new.
